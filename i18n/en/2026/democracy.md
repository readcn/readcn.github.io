# How do Chinese people view democracy?

There is a popular meme on the Chinese Internet: "After democracy, kill your whole family."

Maybe some people find it absurd. However, this is the political reality in China.

The French Revolution shouted liberty, equality, and fraternity. The result? It's the guillotine, it's the hanging of corpses on street lamps, it's the reign of terror that flows like rivers of blood.

More than ten years ago, China’s social network first emerged. Fanatic believers in Western-style democracy even skipped the interlude of fraternity and went straight to the guillotine.

In their eyes, votes are not tools of governance but religious instruments. Anyone who dares to question the turmoil that radical transformation will bring is a slave. The final outcome of the debate is always the curse: after democracy, kill your whole family.

Ordinary people feel chills down their backs when they hear this.

Because in China, using lofty slogans to carry out massacres is not a metaphor, but a blood debt.

People over 60 can hear the accent of 1966.

Few foreigners know that the official banner of the Cultural Revolution was called Great Democracy. The government offices were kicked open, the public security organs were smashed, everyone was free to form associations, and power was seized from the bottom up.

Once the law is destroyed, fanaticism leads directly to animal nature.

Each faction claims to have a monopoly on the truth, and dissenters are mortal enemies. Neighbors harm each other, and flesh and blood are exposed. Within two years, the leaflets turned into live ammunition, and machine guns and artillery were set up on the streets of the city to blast them. Ten years of catastrophe, the mountains and rivers were shattered.

If you look through the historical account books a hundred years further, the ink will become even redder.

In 1836, Hong Xiuquan, a failed scholar, received Christian leaflets on the streets of Guangzhou. After repeated attempts failed, he declared that he was the second son of God and the brother of Jesus, and founded the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom.

The slogan is extremely catchy: Abolition of private property, equalization of wealth and poverty, and all men and women in the world are brothers and sisters. Western missionaries cried with joy, thinking that the gospel had come to the East.

As a result, the kingdom of heaven on earth became a slaughterhouse.

Those who do not believe in religion are all Yama demons. Killing is a crime, but killing monsters is to do justice for heaven. The prosperous land in the south of the Yangtze River turned into scorched earth, the isolated city was trapped for a long time, and the people exchanged their children for food. Hong Xiuquan retreated into the deep palace of Nanjing, kept 88 concubines, and did not leave the palace for more than ten years. If his subordinates made the slightest mistake, they would be wrapped in oilcloth and burned alive, and 20,000 of his own family members would be decapitated due to internal strife among the top officials.

During the fourteen-year jihad, 20 million people were reduced to bones. The death toll exceeded the entire population of Britain at the time and exceeded the combined casualties of the global wars half a century later.

Christian tracts turned into twenty million corpses.
The great slogan of democracy turned into artillery fire on the streets.
Western-style vote frenzy becomes a threat to kill your whole family.

If you understand these three scenes of old events, you can understand the subconscious calculations of the Chinese people:

Democracy = chaos = massacre.

Fanatic slogans that strip away order and law will lead to chaos; and chaos in Chinese history will always end in massacre. Although the signboard has been given a new Western paint finish, a feast of human flesh is still brewing inside.

Besides, the lessons for modern times are at hand.

The Soviet Union collapsed and the oligarchs swallowed them up; the introduction of ballot boxes in Iraq and Libya led to warlords' melee and open-air slave markets. After celebrating the purple ink on voters' fingertips, Western politicians turned around and left, leaving locals with endless car bombs.

Therefore, when Chinese people look at politics, they only look at the results and do not believe in miracles.

1.4 billion mouths consume 700,000 tons of food every day. If the state machinery is shut down for half a month, tens of millions of people will suffer from famine.

The Chinese calculate real accounts:
Can a girl eat barbecue alone on the street at two o'clock in the morning?
Can the high-speed rail and power grid operate on time?
The sewers are blocked, the boss is owed wages, and the clerk is shirksome. Call the 12345 hotline. Can the yamen come to your door to settle the matter within two days?

Compared with politicians who turn around and turn their backs every four years, street lights that are repaired within 48 hours are visible justice.

It is not that the Chinese do not seek fairness or love the rule of law.

Every day on the Internet, voices denounce the overreach of law enforcement, bureaucratic arrogance, food safety hazards, and harsh treatment through hard work and hard work. But what the people want is good governance that abides by the law, listens to public opinion, and does practical things, not a vote-based civil war that splits the country in half.

The most ironic boomerang in history is hitting the other side of the Pacific Ocean.

At that time, the West thought that the Internet would turn China into a moderate Western-style conference hall. In today's algorithmic era, social media has turned Europe and the United States into the most violent Chinese-speaking streets in 2011.

Identity politics has long since swallowed up social consensus. Color, gender, creed, party, trenches divide the people into irreconcilable tribes. Western-style democracy, once regarded as the glue, has now become a machine that creates tears. Politicians do not need to solve problems but only incite hatred; votes no longer bring unity but only create enemies.

Political enemies are no longer compatriots, but national traitors who must be eliminated. Gallows were erected in front of the parliament, fires were raging in the streets, and bullets were flying on the campaign platform. Believers from both parties are shouting: If you want to save democracy, you must first crush the other half of the people.

The Chinese are all too familiar with this drama.

When democracy degenerates into tribal witch hunts, and when votes become a declaration of war to liquidate dissidents, so-called freedom is just a prelude to catastrophe. The road to hell has always been paved with the most holy rhetoric; when fanatics are looking for hemp ropes in the name of democracy, sooner or later the noose will fall on everyone's neck.
